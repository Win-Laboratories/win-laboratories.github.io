## The contemporary source

The earliest source logged so far is a short new-products item, reported as being from *High Fidelity*, July 1974. It calls the Lab 10 "the first audio unit of Win Laboratories of Goleta, Calif." and describes it as follows. The full transcription is in the [Research Notes](/research-notes/#lab-10-turntable-high-fidelity-july-1974-text-checked).

- A manual, single-speed (33 rpm) turntable.
- Driven by dual synchronous motors, "reportedly stable for line voltage between 70 and 125 volts", through soft silicone rubber wheels "without idlers and belts".
- Rumble "rated at −70 dB".
- A low-mass tonearm "of hand-polished wood", whose pivot damping "is said to act as automatic antiskating compensation".
- A white AC pilot light, and a red warning light that comes on when the stylus rests on a stationary disc.
- Price: $150.

Items like this were usually based on the manufacturer's own press release, and the magazine's wording ("reportedly", "is said to") shows it was relaying claims, not testing them. Treat these specifications, and the claim that the Lab 10 was Win Laboratories' first audio unit, as the company's claims as reported in 1974.
